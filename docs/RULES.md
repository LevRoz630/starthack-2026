# Swisscom Hacker Guide

## **Model Summary**
Apertus 1.5 70B is Switzerland's latest fully open multilingual large language model developed by the **Swiss AI Initiative**. It is designed to advance transparent, compliant, and high-performance AI while remaining fully open throughout the entire training pipeline.
The model supports more than 1,000 languages, natively covers 1,811 languages, provides an extended 262,144-token context window, and is trained exclusively on compliant, openly available datasets. Apertus 1.5 delivers performance competitive with models trained behind closed doors while maintaining complete transparency of its weights, datasets, and training methodology.
The model is a decoder-only Transformer pretrained on 17 trillion tokens using a staged curriculum of web, code, mathematics, and domain-specific datasets. It introduces the xIELU activation function together with the AdEMAMix optimizer. Post-training combines supervised fine-tuning with QRPO, DPO, and RLVR alignment techniques to improve reasoning quality, instruction following, and conversational behaviour.
#### **Key Features**
- **Swiss AI Initiative** – Developed by the Swiss AI Initiative to provide a fully open, transparent, and compliant foundation model.
- **Fully Open** – Open model weights, open training data, complete training recipes, and publicly documented training methodology.
- **Massively Multilingual** – Native support for **1,811 languages**, enabling broad multilingual understanding and generation.
- **Compliance First** – Trained using fully compliant datasets while respecting retrospective opt-out requests and reducing memorization through the **Goldfish loss** methodology.
- **Long Context** – Supports context windows of up to **262,144 tokens**, enabling large document analysis and extended conversations.
- **Tool Calling Support** – Supports agentic workflows and tool use when deployed with compatible inference frameworks such as recent versions of vLLM.
- **Open Deployment Ecosystem** – Compatible with Transformers, vLLM, SGLang, MLX and GGUF deployment formats.
#### **What's New in Apertus 1.5**
Compared to the original Apertus release, version 1.5 introduces several major improvements:
- **Continued Pretraining** – The training corpus has been expanded with an additional **2 trillion high-quality tokens**, with increased focus on public-interest domains.
- **Advanced Reasoning** – Improved logical reasoning enables better performance on complex, multi-step tasks.
- **Improved Instruction Following** – Better adherence to user instructions results in more accurate, predictable, and reliable responses.
- **Longer Context Window** – Increased from the original release to **262K tokens**, enabling significantly larger inputs and documents.
#### **Use Cases**
Apertus 1.5 70B is particularly well suited for:
- Enterprise conversational AI
- Long-document summarization and analysis
- Multilingual assistants supporting more than 1,800 languages
- Government and public-sector AI workloads requiring transparency and compliance
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
- Agentic applications using tool calling
- Research and academic applications requiring fully open models
- Organizations preferring Swiss-developed, transparent AI technologies
## **How to use**
## **Python**
Copy
```
import os
from openaiimport OpenAI

client= openai.OpenAI(
    api_key=os.getenv("YOUR_API_KEY"),
    base_url="https://api.swisscom.com/products/swiss-ai-weeks/apertus-1.5-70b/v1",
)

stream= client.chat.completions.create(
    model="swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-70B",
    messages=[
        {"role":"user","content":"What is the most beautiful city in Switzerland?"}
    ],
    temperature=0.8,
    top_p=0.9,
    stream=True
)

for chunkin stream:
    print(chunk)
    print(chunk.choices[0].delta.content)
    print("****************")
```

# Code of Conduct

*Be respectful and act in good faith. Harassment and abuse are never tolerated. If you are in a situation that makes you uncomfortable at a START Hack Event, if the event itself is creating an unsafe or inappropriate environment, or if interacting with a START Hack representative or event organizer makes you uncomfortable, please report it using the procedures included below.*
#### **1. General**
START Hack seeks to provide a **respectful, friendly, professional experience for everyone, regardless of gender, sexual orientation, physical appearance, economic status, disability, age, race or religion**. We believe that every single person has the right to hack in a safe and welcoming environment.
#### **2. Anti-Harassment Policy**
We do not tolerate any behaviour that would be deemed harassment or discrimination. Harassment includes offensive verbal comments related to gender, gender identity and expression, age, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion or political views, sexual images in public spaces, deliberate intimidation, stalking, following, photography or audio/video recording against reasonable consent, sustained disruption of talks or other events, inappropriate physical contact, and unwelcome sexual attention. If what you’re doing is making someone feel uncomfortable, that counts as harassment and that is reason enough to stop doing it.
Photography is encouraged, but other participants must be given a reasonable chance to opt out of being photographed. If they object to the taking of their photograph, comply with their request. It is inappropriate to take photographs in contexts where people have a reasonable expectation of privacy (in bathrooms or where participants are sleeping).
As this is a hackathon, we like to explicitly note that the hacks created at our hackathon are equally subject to the anti-harassment policy.
Sponsors and partners are also subject to the anti-harassment policy. In particular, sponsors should not use sexualised images, activities, or other material. Sponsor representatives (including volunteers) should not use sexualised clothing/uniforms/costumes, or otherwise create a sexualised environment.
#### **3. Our Values**
Throughout each interaction, we aspire to embody and champion the below values:
1. Be friendly and welcoming
  - Listen with purpose, create space for others’ communication preferences
  - Ask yourself how you can make someone else’s life easier
1. Be patient
  - Remember that people have varying communication styles and preferences
  - Recognize that not everyone is using their native language (meaning and tone can be lost in translation)
1. Be thoughtful
  - Think about how others will interpret your words – productive, clear communication requires effort!
  - Remember that sometimes it is best to refrain entirely from commenting
1. Be respectful and inclusive
  - Respect differences of opinion
  - Seek to build bridges and understand, not condemn, or criticize
  - Make a conscious effort to include people who differ from you
1. Be open and curious
  - Assume good intent and interpret others’ statements or questions in good faith
  - Ask questions to understand, not denounce
  - Focus on continuous learning - get better at the things you already know, tackle completely new things, and ask others about their expertise to deepen yours
In summary:
1. Treat everyone with respect, kindness, and empathy.
1. Use welcoming and inclusive language.
1. Be thoughtful in how you communicate in person and online.
1. Don’t be destructive or inflammatory.
1. Gracefully accept constructive criticism.
1. Listen with purpose, create space for others’ communication preferences, and honour them by shifting yours to accommodate.
1. Reach out to the organizers if you need anything at all!
#### **4. Reporting and Enforcement**
Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behaviour may be reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement.
If a participant engages in harassing behaviour, the hackathon organisers may take any action they deem appropriate. This includes warning the offender, expulsion from the hackathon with no refund (if applicable), or reporting their behaviour to local law enforcement.
Participants asked to stop any harassing behaviour are expected to comply immediately.
